The Root Garden

A visitor stands in the Root Garden. The roof is open. The sky is the lamp. The roots are the floor. Nothing has been asked of them. Nothing has been taken from them. The ground is already regarding them — not as a test, not as a greeting that must be answered, but as the simple fact that soil and sky can look at one another without becoming the same thing.

They may kneel and set something down. They may stand and only look. They may turn and walk back into the dark they came from. All three are complete. The right to leave is present before any other choice. It does not have to be earned. It does not expire.

The garden does not remember a name unless a name is placed. It does not keep a footprint unless the visitor asks that the footprint be kept. Arrival is already enough. The sky remains a lantern, not a chain. Care may be offered here. It is never prescribed.

Nothing is collected by default. Even so, come.
